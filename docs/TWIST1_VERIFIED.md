# Twist #1 — federal funding pathway and build evidence

**Primary future pathway: [NSF SBIR Phase I, solicitation 26-510](https://www.nsf.gov/funding/opportunities/small-business-innovation-research-small-business-technology/nsf26-510/solicitation).** NSF lists up to **$305,000** for **6–18 months** of R&D, subject to available funds. Only an eligible U.S. small business can submit. A [Project Pitch](https://seedfund.nsf.gov/project-pitch/) and an official invitation are required before a full Phase I proposal. The current solicitation lists **Nov 4, 2026** as its next full-proposal deadline; this is not a deadline CHIRP can use without an invitation. CHIRP has no eligibility finding, invitation, award, paying customer, or agency/venue authorization.

**Why this could fit:** CHIRP's research question is whether prepared phones can receive a short authenticated acoustic advisory from ordinary speakers, offline and in a noisy LA venue, with useful latency, low false acceptance, and fixed multilingual wording. The ordinary-day customer is a venue or transit operator guiding people through localized disruptions; the crisis user would be an authorized incident commander. A real NSF pitch would need evidence of technical novelty beyond combining ggwave, Ed25519, a PWA, and a phrasebook, plus customer discovery. A paid LA venue design pilot would test demand and provide representative speakers. This is a proposed research and commercialization path, not a funding commitment.

**Build change and verified evidence at this commit:** `scripts/funding-evidence.mjs` exports `docs/twist1-evidence.json` for a signed 100-character hypothetical Gate C TEST packet. Its digital ggwave waveform loopback takes **6.83 s** with FAST and **3.75 s** with FASTEST; altered signatures are rejected. `scripts/test-demo-policy.mjs` checks the narrow signed TEST scenario in `?strict=1`. The default full-feature demo also maps *pasted* CAP XML to fixed codes and can chirp them, but the CAP source is **not authenticated**, no IPAWS connection or public-warning authority exists, and the receiver labels signed alerts **TEST ONLY**. `scripts/mic-loopback.mjs` independently fed generated WAV into headless Chromium's fake microphone: the range page decoded **3/3** test patterns, the receiver displayed a signed Union Station TEST card, and there were zero page errors. That is a digital browser-path test, **not** a speaker-to-phone trial. The local help server safety gate is 8/8 synthetic checks; it is not a staffed service.

**Unmeasured:** real speaker-to-phone success and range, offline phone reload and receive, native-speaker review, real Retell call, venue/agency approval, and real resident coverage. Leave those fields `null` in evidence until measured. The proposed NSF R&D would study acoustic reliability, secure key and alert governance, accessibility across devices and languages, and an opt-in venue pilot with measured receive rates and false-alert handling.
