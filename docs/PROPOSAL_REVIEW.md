# CHIRP — signed local advisories by sound

**Track:** Resilience Communications (Retell AI)  
**Decision seat:** an on-duty operations supervisor at a hypothetical large venue in Los Angeles's Exposition Park  
**First customer hypothesis:** a venue operator that already makes gate and concourse announcements  
**One line:** CHIRP lets a prepared, actively listening phone receive a short, signed, pretranslated **TEST** venue advisory through an audible speaker when mobile data is congested or unavailable. The supervisor decides the action; CHIRP is one delivery channel.

This proposal uses the attached [CHIRP build plan](/Users/ek/Downloads/CHIRP_build_plan.md) as source material. Its build instructions and broad coverage claims are not adopted as requirements. The current product decision follows the user's choice to proceed with CHIRP. **The repo now has a signed TEST packet and a digital waveform loopback, but no measured speaker-to-phone transfer or offline phone result.** The first physical build gate below must pass before the pitch claims an acoustic demo.

**Build status versus proposal:** The current app transmits only phrasebook type 12 (TEST), appended detail 13 (hypothetical Gate C/D scenario), and venue zone 10 with a demo key. English and Spanish are shown, with Spanish unreviewed. Saved cards are reverified from wire, and a help note is local only. The role acknowledgments, withdrawal template, monotonic replay sequence, staffed Retell handoff and real-device test described below are **proposed work, not implemented features**. CAP XML parsing is preview-only and cannot authorize or broadcast an alert.

## The precise decision

**Ordinary Tuesday, 6:40 p.m.:** Gate C's scanners fail before a major Exposition Park event. The venue supervisor records that new admission at C is paused under the venue's own procedure; Gate D staff acknowledge that the alternate is open and accessible; and the safety lead approves that route. Only then does the CHIRP console enable a limited TEST advisory: “Gate C is closed; follow staffed signs to Gate D.” A trained PA operator plays the normal spoken announcement and CHIRP's encoded TEST message. Prepared phones display the same approved wording in the user's chosen language. The console records those decisions; it does not physically close a gate.

**Crisis version at the same site:** mobile-data congestion or a partial network failure and a blocked gate increase crowd pressure. The speaker segment and operator console remain powered and tested; speaker-off is a visible failed state. The same supervisor, safety lead, staffed alternate gate, PA operator, message template, and receiver would have to be in routine use before a real incident. The gentlest action is to **slow** admission to the affected gate and **hand** people to a staffed alternate. CHIRP records the authorizations and carries the advisory; people execute the physical slowdown and handoff. An actual evacuation, all-clear, or public warning stays with the venue's authorized incident command and public alert authorities.

The same software and operational roster could serve both cases. Routine multilingual wayfinding plus incident continuity is the **buyer hypothesis**, not demonstrated willingness to pay. A later deployment could adapt site-specific templates to other LA public venues, shelters, or transit facilities only with their operators' permission and accessibility review. No partnership with the Coliseum, Metro, LA28, LA County, or Retell is claimed.

## Why this is a useful LA problem

Los Angeles County already uses several alert systems, including WEA, Alert LA County, radio/TV EAS, and weather radio. [The County describes these as complementary channels](https://ready.lacounty.gov/emergency-notifications/). CHIRP would add a narrow **on-site** path for prepared receivers; it would not issue County alerts or replace existing public warning. The January 2025 erroneous countywide notification is a caution about governance and downstream delivery, not proof that acoustic proximity would have prevented it: [LA County said the alert left its Emergency Operations Center correctly targeted and spread afterward](https://lacounty.gov/2025/01/10/los-angeles-county-moves-to-immediately-address-emergency-alert-problems-and-implement-solutions/).

The initial reach is people at a participating site with a powered, preloaded phone, microphone permission, and the receiver **open and listening**. A first pilot should provision staff receivers; attendee opt-in is an adoption experiment, not a dependable safety channel. It does not cover every phone in earshot, every attendee, or every LA resident. Audible PA speech, staffed assistance, accessible signs, and the venue's official emergency procedure are parallel channels for people without the app, a working phone, or microphone access; their actual language, disability and staffing coverage must also be measured. Site trials should count **unreached people**, not infer coverage from speaker range. Serving all LA residents would require separate agency distribution and multiple channels; this venue pilot cannot satisfy that goal alone.

## Product flow and safety rules

1. **Prepare before an outage.** The receiver is installed or opened over HTTPS while online. It caches the app, its limited, versioned phrasebook, venue map/area names and trusted TEST public key. The user chooses a language and explicitly taps **Start listening** to grant microphone permission. [Browser microphone capture requires a secure context and permission](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/MediaDevices/getUserMedia); [service-worker caching](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Service_Worker_API) cannot wake a closed app or make a locked phone listen.
2. **Record Slow and Hand before sending.** The supervisor records the pause at Gate C. Gate D staff acknowledge the open, accessible route and the safety lead separately approves it. The console visibly blocks **Publish** if any acknowledgment, speaker check or preapproved phrase is missing; it records who acted and when. The hackathon simulates these roles. No click physically slows a gate or proves that a route is safe.
3. **Approve one limited message.** The supervisor selects a preapproved gate-change or **withdraw route** template and named site/area. The console advances a persistent, monotonic sequence for that site/area, sets issued time and a short validity window (60 seconds in the demo), TEST status and phrasebook version, then signs the exact bytes with a **demo-only** Ed25519 key kept outside the public receiver. The first build exposes no EVACUATE or ALL CLEAR button. Withdrawal says “The previous Gate C route advisory is withdrawn; ask venue staff for directions” and names no unverified alternate. A new route needs fresh staffing and safety sign-off. The message model borrows status, source, area, expiry and update concepts from the [Common Alerting Protocol](https://docs.oasis-open.org/emergency/cap/v1.2/CAP-v1.2-os.html); the prototype is **not CAP compliant**.
4. **Transmit through real sound.** A laptop or speaker plays the signed payload with an audible [ggwave](https://github.com/ggerganov/ggwave) protocol. The receiver decodes locally, checks the signature, site/area, phrasebook version and expiry, then compares the sequence with the highest accepted value persisted for that site/area. It rejects duplicates **and older, still-valid advisories** after a newer one. A forged, expired, wrong-site or unknown-version packet is rejected or clearly marked unusable. Demo phones are clock-synced before disconnecting; if a receiver cannot establish freshness or its clock is outside the permitted window, it fails closed and directs the person to staff. Trustworthy offline time after a restart is a production research question. A receiver that misses a withdrawal can still show the old route until its short expiry; signs and staff remain essential, and the screen labels the time and says to confirm with staff. Hearing a packet does **not** establish the listener's physical location; the screen names the affected area and asks them to confirm they are there.
5. **Keep help status truthful.** If the user taps **I need assistance** while offline, the screen says **saved locally — NOT DELIVERED**. It does not promise background sync, a mesh, a dispatcher, or a response. After connectivity returns, an explicit send action and server acknowledgement can change the status to delivered. The console must show whether staff accepted that handoff. A Retell language-assistance call is an **online** extension only, with a clearly staffed human handoff and no automatic emergency dispatch.
6. **Keep the ordinary channel useful.** The same approved phrasebook covers low-stakes gate changes, accessible service-point changes and queue instructions. Site-specific identifiers and the exact Gate C → D wording in each supported language are provisioned before use. Unknown or mismatched versions fail closed. A new twist cannot silently add a phrase to phones already offline.

**Candidate wire format (to verify in code):** 17 signed bytes: byte 0 magic; 1 protocol version; 2 TEST-only flags; 3 site ID; 4 area ID; 5 approved template/route ID (including withdrawal); 6 phrasebook version; 7–10 issued Unix seconds; 11–12 validity seconds; 13–16 monotonic site/area sequence. Append the 64-byte Ed25519 signature over exactly those 17 bytes: **81 raw bytes**. The stock [ggwave JavaScript encoder takes a string](https://github.com/ggerganov/ggwave/blob/master/bindings/javascript/emscripten.cpp), so a safe first path is base64url text: **108 characters** for 81 bytes, under the [140-byte variable-length limit](https://github.com/ggerganov/ggwave/blob/master/include/ggwave/ggwave.h). This is a proposed encoding, not a tested packet. [ggwave reports roughly 8–16 bytes/s](https://github.com/ggerganov/ggwave), implying about **6.8–13.5 seconds for text payload alone**, before framing or retries. The first technical gate is a signed byte-for-byte round trip through the actual browser binding and two physical receivers. If we later prove a binary-safe path, an 81-byte raw frame could be shorter. Do not replace the signature with a shared secret embedded in the public receiver.

## First-submission working demo: the hard gate

**Minimum proof:** a local console shows supervisor slowdown recorded, Gate D staff acknowledgment, safety-lead approval and speaker check; it refuses to publish if any one is missing. Then a real speaker sends one signed TEST Gate C → Gate D packet to **two preloaded, unlocked, actively listening devices** with cellular/Wi-Fi disabled after the app is cached. One device can be a provisioned staff receiver; the other can model an opted-in attendee. The first shows the approved English phrase, the second its reviewed Spanish equivalent. Send a modified-signature, expired, wrong-site and unknown-version packet through the **same audio path** and show rejection. Send a newer signed withdrawal and then replay the original **still-valid** route through sound: the receiver must reject the old route, including after an offline restart. A separate screen shows an assistance request as **NOT DELIVERED** while offline. If Retell is demonstrated, reconnect first and label it a separate online flow.

**Phone preparation:** serve the receiver from an actual HTTPS origin (or a locally trusted HTTPS origin configured on both phones), record its URL and app version in the README, and open it on each phone while online. Confirm a visible **cache ready** state, load the correct site key and language phrasebook, grant microphone permission by tapping **Start listening**, and synchronize the demo clocks. Then turn off Wi-Fi and cellular, reload that same origin to verify cached startup, keep each phone unlocked with the receiver in the foreground, and transmit from a separate physical speaker. `localhost` on a laptop does not provision a phone. The exact hosting route and device/browser pair remain to be chosen and tested.

**Measure, do not assume:** record device/browser, speaker, protocol, packet bytes, quiet/noisy condition, distance, transmitted duration, time to display, and successes out of ten attempts. Include the failures in the evidence. At least 8/10 decodes at one metre in a quiet room is only a **stage feasibility gate**, not a safety reliability claim. Add a noisy/attenuated path, speaker-off test, and a count of people with no prepared receiver. Validate Ed25519 separately with deterministic known-answer vectors; record each invalid packet's rejection without pretending ten trials quantify cryptographic security. The real result, rather than a guessed latency or stadium-scale claim, goes on the slide. Test the actual judging phones and speaker; keep a recording of the same reproducible flow as a demo backup.

**Venue stop rules:** no authorized supervisor, unstaffed or inaccessible alternate, missing safety sign-off, unknown phrasebook/key, clock uncertainty, or failed speaker check means **no CHIRP publish**. A readable failure state sends the operator back to the venue's existing procedure. The first live acoustic result proves only the tested devices and room; a public safety pilot needs written venue authority, representative noisy-path measurements, adoption evidence, reviewed translations and measured reach across other channels.

**Build order and go/no-go:** spend the first 90 minutes of implementation on a byte-exact signed text packet through the actual browser binding, speaker and one physical receiver. Obtain a two-phone offline receive and screen recording by **9:00 a.m. Los Angeles time on October 17** or stop claiming an acoustic first-submission demo. Then build (2) the authorization workflow with blocked-publication state; (3) rejection cases through audio; (4) offline cache plus language rendering; (5) a Retell language-help flow **after** reconnection if a working account and staffed handoff exist. This last stage strengthens the sponsor-track case but is not a substitute for acoustic proof. A static mockup, a prerecorded phone screen, or two browser tabs passing data internally do not satisfy the acoustic proof. If the audio gate fails, a narrower gate-approval/phrasebook console with ordinary spoken announcements can be submitted **only if it is actually implemented and tested**; remove every offline acoustic claim and recheck track fit. Otherwise there is no working CHIRP demo to claim.

**Possible future federal R&D route:** [NSF America's Seed Fund SBIR Phase I](https://www.nsf.gov/funding/opportunities/small-business-innovation-research-small-business-technology/nsf26-510/solicitation) permits an eligible U.S. small business to seek **up to $305,000 for 6–18 months** of technical R&D, subject to available funds. A [Project Pitch and an official invitation](https://seedfund.nsf.gov/project-pitch/) are required before a full proposal. No company qualification, invitation, award or venue partner is claimed here; the November 2026 full-proposal deadline is not automatically available to a new team. NSF is an **alternative launch pathway**, not cash available for this hackathon build.

**Research question worth testing:** can a compact, authenticated acoustic advisory be delivered through noisy or compressed venue speaker paths with useful latency and a very low false-acceptance rate, while preserving exact, accessible, versioned language content offline? Phase I would compare protocols, payload sizes and failure modes across representative PA chains, devices and languages. Simply wrapping ggwave in a PWA or adding a venue dashboard would **not** meet NSF's high-risk technical R&D requirement. First seek a paid or sponsored venue design pilot, establish customer need and genuine unsolved technical risk, then submit a Project Pitch if the evidence supports it.

**Visible response to the twist:** the prototype should export a reproducible `chirp-evidence.json` that separates simulated security checks from physical sound measurements: device/browser, speaker, protocol, signed payload size, trials, decode successes, latency, invalid-signature and expired-message acceptance, offline-cache check, language/phrasebook version, and undelivered help count. ## Future twists: a controlled adaptation rule

For each released twist: write the new scenario, identify the authorized decision maker, change one input or policy, add a repeatable test, and capture before/after proof. Keep TEST status, signatures, site/area labels and failed-delivery states intact. Potential changes include noisy audio, a missing staff member, a spoofed packet, a new approved site template, or network recovery. A new phrasebook language requires review and redistribution **before** devices go offline. A new public warning type requires agency authorization; it is not a ten-minute code change. Future twists are unknown, so the proposal does not promise to pass them without new evidence.

## Resident reach, business, and responsible use

The direct beneficiaries in the first pilot are **people on-site**, including LA residents and visitors who need accessible or translated wayfinding. The whole site-service plan uses PA speech, signs and staff; the CHIRP app reaches the prepared subset. Adoption at many public facilities could extend the reach, but no single venue channel covers all LA residents. Track opt-in receiver use, language and accessibility gaps, device exclusions, offline decode rate and staff-assisted completions. Count the denominator: event attendance, staff devices provisioned, attendee devices prepared, people actively listening, and people reached through each other channel. Test opt-in through a ticket or entrance QR flow, foreground/battery burden, and whether the operator permits encoded PA audio. Until those numbers exist, scale and buyer value remain hypotheses.

The commercial buyer hypothesis is a venue operator paying for approved multilingual templates, console controls, training, site testing and support. Sound has no per-recipient cellular transmission cost, but translation review, installation, staff and security are real costs. A signature proves control of a key, **not** that an agency approved a message or a route is safe. The TEST private key and public receiver key are demonstration assets only; the private key never ships in the receiver. Production requires authenticated issuers, two-person approval, key rotation/revocation, source-of-truth integration, an approved clock/freshness policy, accessibility review and incident governance. No part of this proposal grants CHIRP public evacuation authority.
